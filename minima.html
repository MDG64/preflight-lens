How to use

Minima compares a METAR and a TAF against the operating minima of each phase of the flight, and says go, marginal or no go.

Reference support only. Values are computed from the internal reference dataset (EASA Easy Access Rules Rev. 24 · FAA eCFR · ICAO Annex 6 / Doc 9365 5th ed.) and are regulatory reference values, not approved operating minima. The opposable minima are those of your operator's OM-B/OM-C and of the charts in force. Always cross-check against the official METAR/TAF source, the current NOTAM/ATIS (LVP status), the MEL/CDL and your crew qualification. Q4 — descent below DA/MDA — is not computable by any tool: it depends on the visual references the pilot acquires.

Operation setup

Five groups describe the operation: framework and aircraft (EASA or FAA, then a type that fills approach category, speed and equipment), limits and alerts, equipment and crew (LVO capability, HUD/EVS, captain's experience), planning and met (the planning increments applied to a forecast), and the FAA specifics when that framework is selected.

Everything set there is a default, remembered between sessions — with one exception: an MEL degradation describes one aircraft on one day, so it is never reloaded. Anything correctable on a phase card is corrected there, not here: the setup holds only what the cards cannot say.

Airfield database

The panel follows the aerodrome in the search field and holds what the computation needs about it: runways and QFU, magnetic variation, lengths, approach and runway lighting, then the approaches and their minima. Picking a runway end applies its lighting class by itself — one column of Table 9 is worth 100 to 200 m of RVR.

The runway end is picked for you, phase by phase, in that order: the ILS runway as long as it takes no more than 5 kt of tailwind; otherwise a runway with a published approach giving at least 6 kt of headwind; and, if the minima do not allow landing there, back to the ILS runway with up to 10 kt of tailwind. A card is only taken if the wind stays within the aircraft limits and the minima let you land on that end. NOTAMs are already in the sort: a closed runway is out, an unserviceable ILS is no longer an ILS. Take-off has no approach — it keeps the best card, then the strongest headwind. Press a card and your choice holds for all five phases.

Reports and time

The two boxes take the raw METAR and TAF. What the parser used is highlighted in place, so you can see what it read. Each phase carries its own time in UTC: the TAF line evaluated is the one in force at that time, changes and temporary groups included.

The five phases

Tabs 01 Take off · 02 T/O alternate · 03 En-route altn · 04 Destination · 05 Dest. alternate. The same weather is not judged by the same rule twice: a take-off alternate answers to one-engine-out range and its own ceiling criterion, an ERA to its planning increments, a destination alternate to the alternate minima of its approach. Pick the role the aerodrome actually plays.

The flight at a glance

Flight (top bar) turns those tabs into one screen: departure, destination and destination alternate side by side, each judged by the same engine as its phase tab — same minima, same reports, same verdicts. No flight-level verdict is computed: each aerodrome answers for its own phase, and weighing the three together remains the pilot's decision. A card opens the phase view of its aerodrome — that phase alone, and the page keeps that single phase whatever aerodrome you type into it: the route slot follows. Home (on the phone) or picking a station on the Map returns to the full five-tab view. The route is remembered between sessions.

That screen is titled Minima, and its bar carries the navigation of the suite: the house goes to the start page of Preflight Lens, and NOTAM, MAP and WEATHER open those pages on the same three aerodromes — the other readings of the route you are looking at, without typing a code again.

A route in the address opens the app straight on this screen — ?dep=LFPG&dest=LFPO&altn=LFOB — which is how Preflight Lens hands its route over.

Reading a card
→
Go — the observed value meets the requirement.
→
Marginal — it holds, but with little left, or on an assumption worth checking.
→
No go — the requirement is not met.

Each tile shows the observed value against the required one, and a grey line underneath naming the rule that produced the requirement — including the planning increment added, when one was. A required value shown in a field is editable: type over it and the verdict follows. The phase verdict is the worst of its tiles.

Q4 — the decision to continue below DA/MDA — is deliberately absent. No tool can compute it: it depends on the visual references acquired.

On the phone

Below 600 px the layout becomes a single column: favourites, ICAO, evaluation time, then one line per phase. The top bar carries Home back to the start screen, the waypoints to the same Minima screen, NOTAM out to Preflight Lens, and Map to its map; the gear opens the same setup and the ? this same page. The engine, the parser and the database are the same — only the layout changes.
